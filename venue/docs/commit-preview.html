Commitment Preview

The venue asks you to sign thirty-two bytes of noise and attach money. This page derives those bytes from the fields you chose, using the six-word preimage the contract will re-hash when you open it.

A preview, not a signer. Nothing is submitted and no wallet is connected. The address stands in for the single-use account an order would be sent from.

What you are committing to

The contract computes keccak256(abi.encode(DOMAIN_ORDER, committer, side, price, qty, salt)). All six are static types, so the encoding is six thirty-two byte words, no length prefix. Grey is zero padding; ink is your order.

fixed by the contract your address the order, and the only part the wire never carries your salt
keccak256 of the 192 bytes above → the id you are signing
Word 1 is the anti-theft word. Your address is inside the hash, so a commitment lifted off the wire and submitted first by someone else still verifies against their reveal only if they can produce your address, which they cannot. Drop that word and the stolen commitment is perfectly valid, which is what makes the omission easy to miss.

04

What the twenty-nine node operators see

Hedera has no public mempool. That is not privacy. A hashgraph event carries transactions rather than hashes, so your body reaches all twenty-nine consensus nodes in full before it is ordered and before any contract runs. No venue policy runs early enough to help. Six of those operators are financial institutions, and at least one trades this asset class on the same network.

      05

      What the bond costs

      A free commitment is a free phantom order: flood the book with sealed orders you never open, move the reference price, pay nothing. The bond prices that. It is posted now and held for the order's whole life. Three ways out.

      walk away, first 5 minutes

      Returned if you cancel before the reveal window opens. The rest is the cancel fee, , which nobody receives. It is retained, because every candidate recipient would gain a reason to want cancellations.

      reveal and rest

      Not returned at reveal. It becomes a performance bond, held while the order rests for up to eight daily rounds, then credited back when the order retires.

      never reveal

      Gone. Once the reveal window closes anyone may call forfeit(id) and take it. It goes to the sweeper, not the venue, so sweeping is somebody's job and the venue gains nothing from a failed reveal.

      06

      What happens if you never reveal

      The two windows are disjoint. Cancel closes exactly when reveal opens: overlap them and a committer who dislikes the market pays the fee instead of the bond, so cancelling beats lapsing at every parameter and the bond stops pricing non-reveal.

      cancel only · 5 min
      reveal window · 30 min
      forfeitable by anyone, forever
      commit lands, bond posted
      reveal opens, cancel closes
      reveal closes, bond sweepable
      Miss the window and there is no appeal path. The commitment stays on chain as a tombstone, the order never existed as far as the book is concerned, and the bond is claimed by whoever runs a sweeper. The venue cannot return it to you, and that is deliberate rather than an oversight.
      07

      Before you sign

      the one thing this transaction does not contain

      Your salt is not in the calldata, not in storage, not recoverable from the id. It exists only where you put it. Without it you cannot reveal, and a commitment you cannot reveal is handed to a stranger . Save it before you sign.

      Nothing is submitted. Pressing it shows the call you would authorise and the reveal you would owe.
