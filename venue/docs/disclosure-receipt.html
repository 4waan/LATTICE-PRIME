Disclosure Receipt

Every venue will tell you what it did with your money. This one tells you what it did with your information: what it published, how precisely, to whom, from when, and how much of that row's budget your action just spent.

ceilingFor · wouldDisclose · spentBits · wouldAfford · breakingSize
Five getters, no new machinery. Every figure below is one of those five reads, each a view over at most two storage slots. The receipt is not a feature bolted onto the venue. It is the venue's own enforcement, printed.
01

What this contract is allowed to say about you

Four rows of the disclosure matrix, which is all an order book touches. The ceiling is a governed parameter under a committed root, so this table is readable off the chain rather than off a prospectus.

Three of the four carry no budget, and that is the published ceiling restated rather than a hole. A budget must satisfy budgetBits < domainBits, and an exact disclosure costs the whole domain, so a single one exceeds any well formed budget on its own. Attaching one to a row the venue publishes exactly would not refuse anything; it would silence that row permanently, on every call, forever. So the venue refuses to publish such a budget at all. The lever against an exact row is a coarser disclosure, not a tighter bound.

The fourth row's two bits are derived, not chosen. Row 15's value domain is the three disjoint windows the book partitions a commitment's life into: cancel, reveal, forfeit. Two bits, so the largest bound the model admits is one, and one cancellation spends the row. See PolicySets.budgetFor.
02

Act, and read the receipt

Three actions, in the order a sealed order lives them. Each one issues a receipt below, newest first. Nothing here reaches a chain: the arithmetic is the venue's own, inlined from the module the test suite pins to the deployed contract.

03

The row that can run out

Row 15 is the order-lifecycle cell below exact. A cancellation announces one bit. The deployed budget is one bit per epoch, so the first row-15 cancellation is announced and the second valid cancellation completes without its venue event.

Published cellSection 7.2 says (agg, EOD). Neither ideal contains the other, which makes this the venue's only incomparable divergence: less per event than the matrix allows, and sooner.
Budget this epochspentBits(15, epoch) against budgetFor(15).budgetBits.
Breaking sizebreakingSize(15, pred). How many observations before the row is gone, which is the number a supervisor asks for.
The second cancellation still happens. An exhausted budget withholds the venue's speech; it never fails the action. The alternative was tried and discarded during this build: charge at emission and revert, and an attacker exhausts a row with cheap disclosing actions until margin calls cannot be recorded and open positions cannot be closed. So the state transition always completes and only the speech is rationed, which produces the one thing an event stream structurally cannot report: an action that occurred and was not announced. A receipt can report it, because the receipt is a read of state rather than a subscription to a log.

What the receipt does not cover

Printed here because a receipt that overstates its own scope is worse than none: it reconciles against nothing.

On a public ledger a contract that can read a number is a contract whose storage anyone can read. That is the same collapse that moved rows 3, 4 and 5 to (exact, imm) in the deployed parameter set: the matrix reached their public cells through a stage where the venue held the value and the public did not, and no such stage exists here. Commit and reveal still moves when the value becomes legible, which is what it buys against the node set before consensus. It does not narrow who. The receipt says so on every line rather than in a footnote.
05

The same receipt, from a venue that cannot print one

Turquoise Plato Block Discovery hides a block order better than this venue hides one, and by a mechanism this venue does not have: an operator who reads every field in the clear and releases a single predicate to a single counterparty. The pre-trade privacy is real, measured from the venue rulebook rather than asserted.

Read the first column as a receipt and it has one line: everything, immediately, permanently. There is no arithmetic to print because there is no bound to print it against, and no getter to read because the quantity was never counted. This is not a criticism of the mechanism. Conditional disclosure is the thing cryptography implements without an operator, and the two produce the same disclosure to the counterparty; they differ only in who must be trusted to compute the predicate honestly. What the operator's column costs is the ability to answer the question afterwards. A venue that removes the operator inherits the obligation to answer it, which is what this page is.

How this page knows. It runs tools/lattice.mjs, the client half of DisclosureLattice, DisclosureBudget and the meter's spend rule, inlined unmodified by tools/gen-receipt.mjs. tools/lattice.test.mjs pins its output to fixed vectors and test/ReceiptVectors.t.sol asserts the same literals against a deployed OrderBook under the published parameter set, including the two-cancellation sequence above. The two halves are checked against each other rather than asserted to agree, so a page that quoted a budget the venue does not charge would fail the suite.
